# Ship 30 for 30 Writing Principles

*Distilled from the Ship 30 for 30 Ultimate Guide by Dickie Bush and Nicolas Cole.*

## Core Philosophy

Digital Writers treat writing like a startup:
- Ship quickly, gather feedback, iterate.
- Practice In Public — build your audience as you write.
- Progress over perfection.

---

## 1. The Hook (Lead-In / Headline)

**This is the most important line.** If readers don't stop, nothing else matters.

A great hook answers 3 questions before the reader clicks:
- **WHO** is this for?
- **WHAT** is this about?
- **WHY** should they read it? (What is the PROMISE?)

Rules:
- Create a **Curiosity Gap** — tell the beginning and end, not the middle.
- **Clear > Clever.** Specific > Mysterious.
- Use numbers when possible: "7 reasons...", "3 frameworks..."
- Never be vague: "A Forgotten Moon" is bad. "Why 90% of B2B PMs Misread Retention Data" is good.

---

## 2. Introduction

Repeat the headline's promise with slightly more detail.

Answer immediately:
- What is this about?
- Who is it for?
- Why should you trust the author (credibility)?

Use the **1/3/1 rhythm** for the intro:
- One strong opener sentence
- Three sentences of context/credibility
- One strong transition into the body

---

## 3. Skimmability = Readability

Readers skim first, then decide to read.

Format for skimmers:
- **H2 subheadings** for each main section
- **Short paragraphs** (1–3 sentences max)
- **Bulleted lists** when enumerating 3+ items
- **Bold** for the single most important phrase per paragraph
- Alternate between long and short sentences (writing rhythm)

---

## 4. Writing Rhythms

Use the **1/3/1** or **1/5/1** pattern for paragraphs:

```
Short strong opener sentence.
Supporting sentence. Clarifying sentence. Reinforcing sentence.
Short strong conclusion sentence.
```

Avoid monotone rhythms:
- BAD: 5/5/5 (everything is the same length)
- BAD: 1/1/1/1/1 (every sentence its own paragraph — reads like caffeine)
- GOOD: 1/3/1 alternating with bulleted lists

---

## 5. Rate of Revelation

Every sentence must push the idea FORWARD. No repeating what you just said.

Ask after each sentence: "Am I adding something new, or am I restating?"

---

## 6. The Central Idea

One essay = one idea. Not ten.

State it clearly in the introduction. Every section should support it.

---

## 7. Narrative Progression

Structure: Problem → Insight → Evidence → Application → Takeaway

- Open with a problem or tension the reader recognizes
- Surface the insight from transcript evidence
- Ground it with specific examples from Lenny's Podcast
- Show how to apply it
- End with a memorable takeaway

---

## 8. Evidence and Grounding

For a Lenny Growth Assistant essay:
- Ground every major claim in transcript evidence
- Attribution format: "According to [Guest] on Lenny's Podcast, ..."
- Use episode title and guest name in citations
- If a claim is editorial inference, mark it clearly

---

## 9. The Takeaway / Conclusion

The final section must answer: "What do I do with this?"

Rules:
- Make it actionable or perspective-shifting
- Short (3–5 sentences)
- Don't introduce new ideas — distill the whole essay into one memorable line

---

## 10. Target Length

- Minimum: 1,100 words
- Target: 1,250 words
- Maximum: 1,350 words

Long enough to be substantive. Short enough to be readable in one sitting.
